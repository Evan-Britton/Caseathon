# Clean baseline: hybrid in-flight energy model

Generated by `python3 scripts/run_baseline.py` (about 1 minute). The model code is in `scripts/hybrid_model.py`. No degradation and no countermeasure are applied: every later experiment is measured against these numbers.

Outputs:
- `metrics.json`: all results and settings (machine-readable)
- `predictions.csv`: every test checkpoint with each model's P50 and P90, seed 0

## What the model does

At a checkpoint every 10 s during a flight, it predicts **how much more energy (Wh) the flight will use before the rotors stop**.

| Part | Method |
|---|---|
| Energy used so far | Physics: Coulomb counting, the integral of voltage × current |
| Time left on the route | Physics: climb + cruise distance left ÷ planned speed + descent. Rates are fitted on training flights |
| Power over that time | ML: gradient-boosted quantile model (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`, the same algorithm as LightGBM) predicting P50 and P90 power |
| Energy needed | predicted power × time left |
| P90 calibration | Split-conformal: the P90 is scaled so that 90% of outcomes on the most recent 20% of training flights fall under it |

**Decision rule:** continue if *energy used + P90 energy needed ≤ usable energy*, otherwise return. Usable energy is a simulated mission budget after the safety reserve. Real flights used 12.9–37.8 Wh of a 99.9 Wh pack, so none came close to empty. Routes are loops back to the launch point, so a return call only counts if it comes before halfway round. After that, finishing the loop is the quickest way home.

Two comparison models use the same checkpoints: **physics_only** (recent 30 s power × time left) and **direct_ml** (the same boosted model predicting remaining energy directly). **hybrid_uncalibrated** shows what the P90 calibration adds.

## Evaluation setup

- 196 cruise flights (routes R1–R7), 2,775 checkpoints. Ground tests and hovers are excluded.
- Three expanding-window chronological folds, split at flight-day boundaries. Every checkpoint of a flight stays on one side. Each fold refits from scratch on all earlier flights.

  | Fold | Train | Test |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 80 flights, 2019-04-07 to 06-29 | 35 flights, 07-01 to 07-09 |
  | 2 | 115 flights, to 07-09 | 49 flights, 07-10 to 07-24 |
  | 3 | 164 flights, to 07-24 | 32 flights, 08-05 to 10-24 |

- Pooled test set: 116 flights, 1,652 checkpoints. Boosted models are repeated over 5 seeds. 95% intervals come from resampling whole test flights (1,000 reps).

## Results

Error is predicted minus actual, so a negative bias means underestimating, which is the unsafe direction.

| Model | MAE (Wh) | 95% CI | Bias (Wh) | P90 coverage | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| physics_only | 2.47 | 2.40–2.54 | −1.72 | 0.90 | 0.89–0.91 |
| direct_ml | 0.46 | 0.39–0.56 | −0.09 | 0.89 | 0.86–0.92 |
| **hybrid** | **0.36** | 0.32–0.41 | −0.06 | **0.92** | 0.89–0.94 |
| hybrid_uncalibrated | 0.36 | 0.32–0.41 | −0.06 | 0.80 | 0.76–0.84 |

Spread across seeds is at most 0.005 Wh MAE, so which flights end up in the test set matters far more than the random seed. Prediction takes about 0.02 ms per checkpoint.

**Decisions at 20 Wh usable** (P90 rule, 116 test flights, 66 of which truly need to return):

| Model | Caught | Unsafe continue (FN) | Needless abort (FP) | Correct continue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| physics_only | 66 | 0 | 50 (100%) | 0 |
| direct_ml | 66 | 0 | 18 (36%) | 32 |
| **hybrid** | 66 | 0 | 13 (26%) | 37 |
| hybrid_uncalibrated | 66 | 0 | 10 (20%) | 40 |

`metrics.json` has the same table for budgets of 16–26 Wh and for a P50 rule.

## What this shows

1. **The ML part earns its place.** Physics alone assumes the last 30 s of power continues. Early in the flight that window still includes the low-power spin-up on the ground, so during the climb it is off by 8.6 Wh on average, mostly underestimating (mean −7.4 Wh). To stay safe, its calibrated P90 has to be so wide that it aborts every flight. The hybrid's climb error is 0.48 Wh.
2. **Hybrid beats direct regression** (0.36 vs 0.46 Wh). The gap is largest in the latest period, Aug–Oct (fold 3: 0.48 vs 0.65 Wh), which supports the claim that anchoring on physics helps under conditions the model hasn't seen.
3. **Raw quantiles were overconfident on later flights.** The uncalibrated P90 covered only 80%. Calibration restores about 90%, at the cost of 3 more needless aborts at 20 Wh. That is a real safety-versus-usefulness tradeoff.
4. **On clean data, no flight is wrongly told to continue.** That is the point of a reference: unsafe continues are what degradation should cause and what the countermeasure has to prevent.

## Limits to carry forward

- **Performance already drifts in the real timeline.** Hybrid MAE is 0.32 Wh in July but 0.64 in September and 0.78 in October, and P90 coverage falls to 0.73–0.78. Only 11 test flights fall in Sep–Oct, so treat this as a lead, not a finding. It's a candidate "unseen period" for testing the countermeasure.
- The usable-energy budget and the halfway deadline are simulation choices, not measured battery limits.
- The anemometer reading mixes wind with the drone's own motion. There is no true airspeed or temperature, and no battery ID, so the data can't support claims about battery aging.
- Planned route length is a mission-plan parameter the operator knows before take-off. The data has no plan, so it is approximated by the median distance flown on that route, over all flights, test flights included. On R1 (182 flights) one flight barely moves the median, but routes R2, R3 and R4 have one flight each, so there the median *is* that flight's own distance, which sets its distance left, route fraction and halfway deadline. This is a mild leak confined to those few flights: R2–R4 only ever fall in training here, and the test flights it touches are R6's 5 and R7's 2 (whose median is the average of its two flights), 7 of 116.
- Mostly one route (R1, 182 of 196 flights) and one aircraft.
- 185 flights show small negative current readings near zero at rest. This is a defect already in the source, left as is; it is not an injected one.

## Reproduce

Requires Python 3.12+ with numpy, pandas and scikit-learn (this run used Python 3.14.6, numpy 2.4.6, pandas 3.0.5 and scikit-learn 1.9.0).

```bash
gunzip -k data/raw/cmu_drone/flights.csv.gz
python3 scripts/run_baseline.py
```
