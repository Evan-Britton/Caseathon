# Countermeasure results

Generated by `python3 scripts/run_countermeasure.py` (about 4 minutes; the 5 seeds run in parallel). The fix is in `scripts/countermeasure.py`. The degradations and the simulated timeline are the same as in `results/degradation/`.

Outputs:
- `metrics.json`: summary results and settings
- `per_window.csv`: one row per condition × fix × seed × window, including data health fields
- `audits.csv`: every source audit the fix ran, with the estimated bias and the action taken

**Everything here runs on a simulated timeline.** With and without the fix are compared on the same windows, flights and seeds.

## The fix, in two layers

**Layer 1: data health gate (before each retrain).**
- **Reference flights.** Each window, 3 flights (about 12%) are logged by *both* the accurate logger and the cheap feed, and uploaded by a manual path that keeps working when the automatic upload breaks. Their accurate record becomes trusted training data.
- **Source audit.** For each window, the cheap feed's mission energy is compared with the accurate logger's on that window's reference flights, using a 95% t-interval on the ratio.
  - Clearly off 1 → **recalibrate** that window's cheap-feed records.
  - Consistent with 1 → **keep** them.
  - Too few flights, or inconclusive → keep them at **weight 0.2** (unverified).
  - **No records are discarded.**
- **Freshness check.** It tracks how old the newest automatic upload is, so an outage gets flagged.

**Layer 2: decision safeguards.**
- **Trusted calibration.** The P90 is calibrated on held-out recent *trusted* flights (reference flights and accurate-logger flights), never on the feed that might be biased.
- **Health badge.** Red when unverified records carry more than 10% of the training weight. Amber when the feed was recalibrated, the upload is stale, or there's nothing trusted to calibrate on. Green otherwise.
- **Operator review.** CONTINUE if even the P90 fits the budget, RETURN if even the median doesn't, **DEFER** when the band straddles the budget. When health is red, a continue with less than 1 Wh to spare is also deferred. Deferred flights are scored as returned (the safe choice) and counted as human workload.

The per-window audit follows the drifting bias closely: its estimates go 0.97, 0.94, 0.91 … 0.82, matching the true values. An over-reading feed, the opposite defect, makes the unprotected system ground nearly half the flights it could have completed (145 needless aborts). The fix cuts that to 78.

## What this shows

1. **The fix restores the safety margin.** In the combined condition, P90 coverage goes from 51% back to 92% and MAE from 0.84 to 0.41 Wh, about the clean level. Unsafe continues drop from 4.0 to 0.4.
2. **It doesn't break ordinary conditions.** On clean data the fixed system matches the unprotected one (0.39 Wh, 66 vs 65 aborts), and the badge stays green in all 35 windows.
3. **It holds on defects it wasn't designed around:** a larger bias, a drifting bias, an offset, and a bias in the opposite direction. All four land at 0.41 Wh and 92–93% coverage.
4. **It fixes the link where the chain breaks.** The poisoned feed taught the ML part the wrong power. The audit corrects those records before training, and trusted calibration re-checks the margin against ground truth.
5. **Nothing is thrown away.** No records are discarded. The cheap feed's records are corrected, not removed.

## Costs, failures and tradeoffs

- **It depends on reference flights.** Without them, the bias can't be measured. The automatic rule improves only partly (coverage 63%, 2 unsafe continues). Operator review removes the unsafe continues only by deferring 174 decisions (about 24%) and raising aborts to 100. **This is the main limit:** reference flights are the fix.
- **Reference flights cost about 12% of flights.** Each needs dual logging and a manual upload.
- **Aborts rise compared with the poisoned system** (39 → 76 in the combined condition). The poisoned system's low abort count was false confidence; the fixed system sits near the clean level (65). It still makes about 11 more needless aborts than clean data, because its calibration set is smaller.
- **Residual risk.** Unsafe continues are not exactly zero: 0.4 per run on average in the stale and combined conditions, about 2 events across 5 seeds.
- **The stale-only condition gains little** (0.47 → 0.46 Wh, 97 → 94 aborts). The physics anchor already protected it. The fix's main contribution there is flagging the outage (amber in 20 of 35 windows).
- **A rare-event tradeoff.** On the single 750 g flight, the fix's P90 covers less than the unprotected model in the clean condition (61% vs 90%), and in the stale condition (80% vs 99%). In the combined condition it raises coverage from 33% to 71% and cuts error from 1.47 to 0.46 Wh. Trusted calibration with fewer flights fits the typical flight better than the outlier. This is only one flight, so treat it as a warning, not a measurement.
- **Operator workload.** About 9–12% of flight-budget decisions are marginal and go to the operator (66–105 of 732), rising to 24% in the failure case. Human review time hasn't been measured yet; it needs a walkthrough with a timer.
