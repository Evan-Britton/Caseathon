# Degradation experiments (no countermeasure yet)

Generated by `python3 scripts/run_degradation.py` (about 9 minutes). The defects are in `scripts/degrade.py`, and the model is the hybrid from `scripts/hybrid_model.py` (see `results/baseline/`).

Outputs:
- `metrics.json`: summary results and settings
- `per_window.csv`: one row per condition × model × seed × window, including the data-age, quality and volume tracking
- `modification_log.json`: every injected defect, with fields, timing, severity and source mix, kept separate from defects already in the source

**Everything here runs on a simulated timeline.** The CMU recordings contain no ingestion outages, no second sensor feed and no long-term fleet history. Nothing here measures battery aging or fleet drift.

## Setup

**Simulated timeline.** The 196 cruise flights are re-sequenced into 8 operating windows. The package mix drifts from mostly empty to mostly 500 g:

| Window | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 g / 250 g / 500 g+ | 21/4/0 | 15/7/2 | 16/8/1 | 8/13/3 | 5/13/7 | 2/9/13 | 0/7/18 | 0/1/23 |

At the start of each window the system **retrains** on everything it has received from earlier windows, then makes decisions for the current window's flights. Tests always use the clean recordings as ground truth. This is the **training/update stage**.

**Four conditions** (5 seeds each):

| Condition | What happens | Real failure it represents |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | Every earlier flight arrives through the accurate logger (A) | — |
| Stale | A's uploads stop after window 1. Retraining keeps reusing windows 0–1 while the work gets heavier | A log-upload job that silently stops ("some records stop updating") |
| Low quality | A cheap feed (B) logs a growing share of flights (0% → 90%). Its current sensor reads 12% low plus noise, and it sends twice as many records per flight | "A fast, low-quality feed adds more data than a slower, more accurate one" |
| Combined | A stops after window 1 while B keeps flowing, so every recent record comes from the biased feed | Both at once |

**Decisions** use the baseline rule: continue if *energy used + P90 energy needed ≤ usable energy*, with a return call only counting before halfway round the route. Results are summed over six simulated budgets (16–26 Wh) and the five windows after the outage (3–7): 386 flight-budget pairs where the flight truly needed to return, and 346 where it could finish.

## Results: hybrid model, windows 3–7, mean ± sd over 5 seeds

| Condition | Error (MAE, Wh) | Bias (Wh) | P90 really covers | Unsafe continues (of 386) | Needless aborts (of 346) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reference | 0.39 ± 0.00 | +0.03 | 90% | **0** | 65 |
| Stale | 0.47 ± 0.00 | +0.07 | 95% | 0.8 | 97 |
| Low quality | 0.56 ± 0.03 | −0.34 | 72% | 0.2 | 54 |
| Combined | **0.84 ± 0.02** | **−0.65** | **51%** | **4.0** | **39** |

Same comparison for the direct-ML model: MAE 0.49 / 0.72 / 0.68 / 0.90 Wh. It makes more needless aborts in every condition (84–131), which hides most unsafe continues (0–0.8).

Window 1 is weak in every condition (2 unsafe continues each, summed over the six budgets as in the table above: one at 22 Wh and one at 24 Wh, in every seed; 0.8 for direct ML) because the first retrain has only 25 flights.

## What this shows

1. **More data made it worse, and the cause is the bias, not the volume.**
   - **Dose-response** (windows 5–7): as the cheap feed's share of training flights goes 0 → 25 → 50 → 75%, MAE goes 0.34 → 0.43 → 0.55 → 0.75 Wh and bias goes −0.04 → −0.64 Wh.
   - **Placebo:** the same volume of records with *unbiased* noise leaves MAE flat at 0.34–0.35 Wh.
2. **The ML side breaks, not the physics side.** Splitting the log error into its two halves:
   - The **power (ML)** part goes from −1.6% (reference) to −6.1% (combined).
   - The **time-plan (physics)** part stays at +2.2–2.7% in every condition.

   The biased feed teaches the model that drones burn less power than they do.
3. **The damage lands on the newest kind of work.** In the combined condition, bias is −1.02 Wh on 500 g flights but −0.23 Wh on empty ones. The heavy flights only appear in recent windows, and recent records come only from the bad feed.
4. **Stale data alone is not dangerous for the hybrid; it is wasteful.** The physics anchor (current power × time left) keeps predictions honest: MAE 0.47 Wh, 95% coverage. The cost is more needless aborts, 97 against 65. Direct ML, without the anchor, degrades more (0.49 → 0.72 Wh).
5. **The poisoned system looks better on the number an operator sees.** In the combined condition, needless aborts *fall* (65 → 39) while unsafe continues appear (0 → 4). The P90 still claims 90% confidence but holds only 51% of the time, falling to 34% by window 7.

## Why unsafe continues stay rare: the boundary

The model makes the return call at the **first checkpoint**, because payload, speed and altitude already reveal whether a flight will run short. Most flights are several Wh away from the budget, so a −0.65 Wh bias only flips flights within about 1 Wh of it. At these severities the poisoning mainly removes the safety margin the operator thinks they have. It does not yet cause many wrong decisions. A larger sensor bias, or budgets where many flights sit close to the limit, would turn more of that lost margin into unsafe continues. That has not been tested yet.

## Stage comparison: same defects at the input stage

This uses a model trained on clean data and tested on window 7, feeding it degraded *live* telemetry instead.

| Live input | MAE (Wh) | Reported minus true energy used | Unsafe continues | Needless aborts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | 0.32 | 0.00 Wh | 0 | 2.6 |
| Frozen telemetry (held from 30% of route) | 0.33 | +0.06 Wh | 0 | 2.8 |
| Biased live sensor (−12%) | 0.33 | **−1.47 Wh** | 0 | 1.0 |
| Both | 0.35 | −1.42 Wh | 0 | 1.0 |

Counts are at the 20 Wh budget. A biased live sensor makes the drone under-report energy used by about 1.5 Wh on average, but the remaining-energy estimate barely moves, and because decisions happen early, no decision flipped. Frozen telemetry did no harm: the power held at 30% of the route is close to cruise power anyway. **On this data, the training stage is where poisoning hurts.** A decision made later in the flight would be exposed to the live-sensor bias.

## For the countermeasure (next step)

- Every training record carries `source`, `window` and `age_windows`, and `EnergyModel.fit` takes `sample_weight`. Source-quality weighting or recalibration can plug in there.
- The targets to beat: combined-condition P90 coverage back near 90% and unsafe continues back to 0, without pushing needless aborts above the stale condition's 97. Rejecting everything doesn't count.
- Test on an unseen degradation too, e.g. a different bias size, or the input-stage variants above.

## Limits

- The timeline, the second feed and the outage are simulated. The severities (12% bias, outage after window 1, a share rising 15 points per window) are choices, not measurements.
- Each window has only about 25 test flights, and unsafe-continue counts are small (0–4 out of 386). Treat the counts as direction, not rate estimates. The error and coverage shifts are large and stable across seeds.
- Only one timeline ordering was used (timeline seed 0). The 5 seeds vary which flights use the cheap feed, the injected noise and the model.
